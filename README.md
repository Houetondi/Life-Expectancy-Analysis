# Project Title
Analyzing the Socioeconomic and Health Factors Influencing Life Expectancy Across Countries. 

1- Project Type Flags
Data Cleaning / Wrangling
Outlier Detection & Treatment
Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
Statistical Analysis & Correlation Assessment
Dashboard / Data visualization
Predictive Analytics & Machine Learning Modeling

2- Project Overview 
The goal of this project is to analyze global health, economic, and social data to identify the factors that most strongly influence life expectancy and to build a model that can predict life expectancy with higher accuracy. By including health indicators as well as additional variables such as environmental quality, healthcare access, poverty, nutrition, and lifestyle behaviors, the project aims to provide a deeper understanding of what drives differences in longevity across countries.


3- Problem Statement
What are the key factors that most influence life expectancy, and how can identifying them help policymakers create targeted interventions to improve population well-being? This problem is significant because life expectancy is one of the most widely used measures of a population’s overall well-being. Understanding which factors most affect life expectancy can help policymakers design evidence-based interventions, such as improving air quality, increasing access to clean water, expanding healthcare coverage, or reducing smoking rates.

4- Significance
Understanding the determinants of life expectancy is crucial for improving population health. Solving this problem can lead to more equitable health outcomes and improved global well-being.

5- Research Question
Does higher GDP correlate with higher life expectancy?
Does higher alcohol consumption negatively impact life expectancy?
Is there a relationship between adult mortality and life expectancy?
Does increased schooling lead to higher life expectancy?
Is HIV/AIDS prevalence negatively associated with life expectancy?

6- H0 (Null Hypothesis)
There is no correlation between GDP and life expectancy.
Alcohol consumption has no effect on life expectancy.
Adult mortality is not related to life expectancy.
Schooling has no impact on life expectancy.
HIV/AIDS prevalence does not affect life expectancy.

7- H1 (Alternative Hypothesis)
There is a positive correlation between GDP and life expectancy.
Higher alcohol consumption is associated with lower life expectancy.
Higher adult mortality is associated with lower life expectancy.
Higher levels of schooling are associated with higher life expectancy.
Higher HIV/AIDS prevalence is associated with lower life expectancy.

8- Rationale
GDP reflects a country’s economic strength, which can influence healthcare quality and access.
Excessive alcohol use is linked to various health issues that may reduce life expectancy.
Adult mortality directly affects the average lifespan in a population.
Education can improve health awareness and access to better jobs and healthcare.
HIV/AIDS is a major health concern that can significantly reduce life expectancy.
